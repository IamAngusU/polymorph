## Open-source use

The public license is GNU AGPL v3 only.

AGPL permits commercial use, private use, modification and redistribution, subject to its conditions. In particular, modified versions that support remote network interaction must offer their remote users access to the Corresponding Source as required by section 13.

Redistributed copies and modified versions also carry the notice, source and licensing obligations described by the AGPL. Read the license for the exact rules. This document is not an attempt to compress a software license into three optimistic bullets.

## Commercial use

Commercial use is **not prohibited** by the public license.

If your organization can comply with AGPL-3.0-only, you do not need to buy a separate license merely because money is involved.

A separate commercial license is available for organizations that need different terms, for example:

- proprietary or closed-source use
- embedding Polymorph into a product that cannot comply with AGPL copyleft obligations
- operating a modified network service without the AGPL source-offer obligations
- redistribution under negotiated proprietary terms
- white-label or additional branding rights
- contractual support, warranty, indemnity or other negotiated obligations

Those alternative rights exist only when agreed in a separate written agreement.

The commercial license is an alternative license, not a tax on using open source. If the AGPL terms work for your use case, use the AGPL. Surprisingly efficient.

The software license and project branding are separate things.

The AGPL licenses the covered software. It does not turn the Polymorph name or project logo into a permission to make a modified fork look official or endorsed.

## Contributions and dual licensing

Polymorph may be offered under separate commercial terms in addition to AGPL-3.0-only.

That only works cleanly while the project has the rights needed to grant both paths. For that reason, substantive third-party code is not merged until a contributor agreement grants the maintainer sufficient rights for the public AGPL release and alternative commercial licensing.

A pull request is useful. It is not, by itself, a copyright assignment or a complete dual-licensing agreement.

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) before sending code.
